Even though I started working on this book (the title and more to be revealed soon) in 2019, I have been writing for quite a while, to say the least. If we’re pressing back on the time machine and holding that button down, the first non-assignment thing I remember writing was a creative spin on the Nativity using full poetry for each line. I was 12.

For a quick background on why I would think that would be a good idea, my Aunt Kathleen (may she rest in peace), or Mrs Celestine as I called her on school grounds, was the principal of our primary school, and as an accomplished pianist and musical savant, she had the entire school participate in a musical for Christmas every year instead of end-of-term exams. I’m talking full stage productions with auditions, rehearsals, scripts, lighting, stage crew and stages. If you weren’t acting, singing or both, you were in the back as part of the stage crew. I guess that's where I got the confidence to submit an unsolicited script to the church I attended for use in Christmas Mass. Somehow, I fully expected a group of volunteer, amateur actors, some north of seventy years old, to learn poetic verses one week before what was really an off-the-cuff reenactment between the first and second reading. Needless to say, I got a few curious looks and gentle pats on the back, but they didn't use my script.

After that, I dabbled in poetry. I don’t think I was ever good at it. I might have read something at an early, early edition of UWI Speak that was more like a hip-hop verse and felt very proud. I did get booed on stage at a competition in UWI (Mr. Uwiverse, I think it was called) mainly because I went up to recite a poem with a piece of paper in hand. I didn’t really feel any shame or any embarrassment in the moment, just only a “well, this sucks” type ah vibe. It led me to dive into music as a writer and performer, since I reasoned that performing a song would have drastically reduced the chances of my poor reception.

I had considerably more success writing scripts for plays. I wrote a couple for my friend Rueanna, and they were performed and did well on Cave Hill campus, or so I was told. I wrote and directed one that was performed for the Milner Hall concert one time. I think the concert was called “Breathe”. Many doubted I could pull it off since I wasn’t really known in creative circles like that and worse yet, I was doing Comp Sci…not Humanities.

It was a complete surprise to many except me (I was real confident about it) when it was overwhelmingly well received and boosted my confidence significantly. I led a youth group called the CYC (Creative Youth Company) and wrote a couple of plays for it. Some cable station still occasionally broadcasts a performance of me with the youth group that I’m not too inspired to share lol.

My sister Paula often directed and acted in my plays, and around 2007 we collaborated on a musical called “My Blessed Lady” (I had to look this up). I wrote the musical and most of the songs, while she directed, which worked well for us in a few church plays. It was genuinely amazing and was my proudest moment as a writer at that time. This year was a nice full-circle moment for me to attend her musical, “Arts Academy”, which she wrote and directed, and I was overjoyed to witness the outstanding reception it received.

After that, it was mostly me as a lyrical writer within Trinidad hip hop or as we would say, trini hip hop. Oh shucks, I getting ahead of myself. I also got into blogging.

In February 2008, according to the archives, I started a blog called “[Underground Trini Artiste](https://undergroundtriniartiste.blogspot.com/)” on Blogger, one of the few platforms that Google hasn’t discontinued after a few years. This continued alongside my artistic pursuits and I built up a decent following until being an artist took over. I had a short stint writing for [Trinipulse.com,](http://trinipulse.com) which was mostly memorable because it was the first time I was paid to write. The owner was also a pretty cool, straight-up dude.

I’m trying to remember when I started writing for [outlish.com](http://outlish.com). Probably 2010? My first article “[The Art of Tiefin a Wine](https://www.outlish.com/the-art-of-tiefin-a-wine/)” is funny but did not age well at all, so don’t come for me lol. And don’t follow that advice haha, I will not be held liable. Outlish was an online magazine founded by Karel McIntosh and it was definitely ahead of its time. It had a very polished design for that era and many talented writers contributed over its run.

Of course, I was mostly writing music for myself. This is from 2009 onwards. I did pretty well within the modest scope of trini hip hop and I suppose most people who know me for anything creative know me from that. It wasn’t until 2016 that I restarted writing articles online, mostly to promote my newly opened recording studio, Future Crab Studios. Some of those articles would go on to be pretty popular. I even went viral for the wrong reason with a top 100 soca artists of all time list.

It would be quite in 2019 before I commenced writing anything that would resemble writing a book. If you’ve made it this far, here is the recap: I’ve written poetry (badly), a couple plays, a musical, many blog articles and songs. I hadn’t even written a short story since primary school. I suppose I was good at it then, but I looked at it as practice for the Common Entrance exam. I didn’t do it in my spare time. Aunty Kathleen, also my English teacher, would admonish me for repurposing Hardy Boys plots for my essays, leaning heavily on tropes I enjoyed from those books. Every protagonist had to sprint from an attacker at the start of the chapter before being knocked out, preferably by chloroform.

Before then, I had placed writing a book firmly on my bucket list. Like something I would pick up when I turned fifty. Ironically, while I’m not there yet, I’m not that far off either. Anyway, it would be the death of a friend of mine that would push me to start and the same book I’m working on now started in 2019.

Now, it wouldn’t be true to say that it took seven years to write the book. I did take roughly two years off. But it's fair to say it took me at least four years to understand how to write a book properly. A side quest into short story writing helped a lot, and it was there that I would experience two major successes.

In 2022, I was shortlisted in the Island Voices competition, which led to a placement in a short story compilation also called “[Island Voices](https://www.hachettelearning.com/english/island-voices)”. This was the first time (and so far the only time) I was published, so yuh boy was very proud. In 2023, I won a short story competition held by [Caribbean Magazine Plus](https://www.stvincenttimes.com/david-hamilton-wins-caribbean-magazine-plus-short-story-2022-contest/). Again, I was very proud, but this time I was something even better; I was paid. In US dollars too, so I felt like a real bite up shilling.

2024 was a challenging year, to say the least and later in the year I embarked on a project that you will hear about soon. That undertaking required me to put the book aside for a while. In retrospect, that sabbatical was one of the best things I could have done for this project. It not only gave me perspective, but also allowed me to understand the craft and structure of writing a book. Before that, I was doing madness like trying to polish every scene to publication level when, in reality, that process should be reserved for after the central plot and flow of the book are locked in.

So now in 2026, where am I at? Right now, I am locked in on finishing this book. I would say the developmental edit is complete, but I still have a lot of polishing to do. As someone who used to read a LOT of books growing up, I have very high standards; the challenge is meeting them. The good news is that the book has improved dramatically over the three months I have resumed work on it, and I can see the trajectory toward it becoming something I can be proud to share with the public.

Do I have any advice? Just start yes. And keep going at it. My cadence was one hour before work, five days a week. After three years, I had something I could work with. So just start, don’t overthink it.


And that’s my entire writer’s journey. Not even just for the book lol. That would be a separate long story. Next week, I will share some details about the book itself. Till then, I hope you enjoy your week.
